The 25 times rule prices year one of thirty

The number people carry out of the 4 percent rule is 25 times annual spending: $40,000 of withdrawals needs a $1,000,000 portfolio. The rule itself indexes withdrawals to inflation every year, but the mental number stays at year one. Year 10 of that same plan withdraws $52,191. Year 25 withdraws $81,312, more than double the year-one figure the multiple was priced on.

Add the column up. Flat $40,000 for 30 years costs $1,200,000. The same $40,000 indexed at 3 percent costs $1,903,017, nearly twice the starting balance. The $703,017 difference is the inflation bill, and it appears nowhere in the save-25-times sentence. A plan that checks survival against flat withdrawals is testing a cheaper plan than the one that gets lived.

The year-25 gap alone is $41,312, which is more than the entire first year of spending. That is one year's difference between what the retiree planned to pull and what prices force. Bad sequence years get blamed on markets; this gap is arithmetic. It shows up in every history including the calm ones, and it compounds in the same direction every single year.

Survival checks are only honest when the withdrawals they test are the indexed ones.
